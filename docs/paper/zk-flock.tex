\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Problem.} Flock is a hash-based SNARK for batch R1CS over $\Ftwo$ --- a
zerocheck + lincheck PIOP compiled with a ring-switched Ligerito commitment and
Fiat--Shamir. It is succinct but not zero-knowledge: every prover message is a
deterministic function of the witness.
\textbf{Construction.} We give a candidate honest-verifier zero-knowledge mode
for BLAKE3 batch statements from three ingredients: randomizer witness rows that
blind the affine transcript classes; a hiding Ligerito opening (a low-half mask
plus a full-support blinder folded into the recursion); and a degree-2 committed
mask channel $\gamma\cdot P\cdot Q$ for the one class the first two miss --- the
zerocheck round messages, whose leading coefficient no degree-1 mask can reach in
characteristic~2.
\textbf{Claim.} For the enumerated certified configurations of
Table~\ref{tab:configs}, the \emph{algebraic} transcript of the reference prover
is statistically witness-indistinguishable up to an explicit rank-failure term
$\erank$, and the complete protocol is \emph{computational} zero-knowledge in the
classical random-oracle model, because hiding of unopened Merkle siblings is
computational.
\textbf{Implemented.} The amended prover and verifier run end to end and verify;
the optimized prover is \emph{not} amended and is excluded from the claim.
\textbf{Evidence.} The reduction to witness-indistinguishability is proved; the
masking, triangular-composition and bad-set mixture theorems are machine-checked
in Lean~4; and the composition hypotheses are discharged by exact $\Ftwo$
image-coverage certificates computed on the real prover --- including a
complete-transcript joint conditional certificate at the certified fixture, in
which no claim-preserving witness direction escapes the joint mask image.
Per-item status is tabulated in Table~\ref{tab:claims} and
\S\ref{sec:roadmap}.
\textbf{Assumptions and limits.} A random-oracle (or hash-pseudorandomness)
assumption for sibling hiding; certificates are computed at sampled challenge
tuples; the full-transcript certificate is established at a reduced-size fixture
with structural transfer to production size; no independent cryptographic review
has been performed. QROM security is not claimed.
\textbf{Performance.} Reference-path and optimized-path costs are reported
separately in \S\ref{sec:perf}; they are different pipelines and are not
comparable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, claim, and status}\label{sec:scope}

This section is the only place scope and status are stated, apart from the
limitations in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Statement family.} BLAKE3 \emph{batch} statements only: $2^{n}$
independent compressions sharing per-block matrices. The SHA-256 and Keccak
encoders and the hash-chain statement remain non-zk, and the chain statement is
excluded on purpose --- it binds public endpoints, which breaks the simulator
argument of \S\ref{sec:relation}. This is not a generic ZK transformation for
Flock.

\paragraph{Object of the claim.} The \emph{reference} amended prover
(\texttt{prove\_zk\_a1}; see Appendix~\ref{app:impl}), which runs the masked
zerocheck. The optimized single-pass prover runs the un-amended zerocheck; the
round-message hiding argument does not apply to it, and no zero-knowledge claim
is made for it. The zero-knowledge API is certificate-gated: a configuration
without a checked certificate is refused, not proved.

\paragraph{Property.} The algebraic transcript --- every verifier-visible field
element --- is statistically witness-indistinguishable up to $\erank$
(\S\ref{sec:certificates}). Unopened Merkle siblings are hidden
\emph{computationally} under the hash assumption of \S\ref{sec:assumptions}.
Composing the two, the complete protocol is computational zero-knowledge in the
classical ROM after Fiat--Shamir. ``Honest-verifier'' is genuine: the argument is
public-coin, so verifier aborts and malformed challenges are not a separate case.
Masks are drawn freshly per proof, so $k$ proofs compose with error
$k(\erank+\ehash)$.

\paragraph{QROM.} Not claimed, for either zero-knowledge or soundness. The
simulator programs nothing and rewinds nothing, which makes a QROM
zero-knowledge statement plausible, but we do not write the theorem and do not
rely on it.

\paragraph{Status.} \textbf{Experimental candidate with a proven partial core.}
Several load-bearing steps are discharged by exact computation rather than
closed-form proof, one composition hypothesis is measured rather than derived,
and there has been no independent review. We do not label this production
zero-knowledge.

\section{The relation, and what zero-knowledge means here}\label{sec:relation}

\begin{definition}[Statement and witness]
A BLAKE3 batch statement is the public tuple $x = (\text{batch size } 2^{n})$;
everything else about the instance --- the sparse matrices $A,B$, the witness
layout, the randomizer layout --- is determined by $n$ and the protocol version
and is absorbed into a statement digest. The witness is a packed assignment
$z\in\Ftwo^{2^{m}}$, and the relation is
\[
  \mathcal{R} \;=\; \{(x,z)\;:\; A z \circ B z = z\},
\]
an R1CS over $\Ftwo$ with $C = I$, block-diagonal over the batch.
\end{definition}

Three facts about this relation drive everything else. \emph{First}, no
caller-supplied value is bound: not a message, not a chaining value, not a hash
output, not a compression output. The verifier receives the setup, the
commitment, and the proof, and compares the accepted claim values against
nothing. The only pinned wire is the constant-one wire, pinned to the constant
$1$. \emph{Second}, a valid witness is therefore efficiently self-generatable:
choose arbitrary compression inputs, run the honest compression trace, fill the
randomizer rows with uniform bits. \emph{Third}, the simulator receives $x$
alone.

\begin{definition}[View and distance]
$\mathrm{View}\langle \mathsf{P}(x,z),\mathsf{V}(x)\rangle$ is the transcript
together with the verifier's coins. $\Dsd(X,Y)$ denotes statistical distance.
The protocol is honest-verifier zero-knowledge with error $\varepsilon$ if there
is a probabilistic polynomial-time $\mathsf{S}$ with
$\Dsd(\mathrm{View}\langle \mathsf{P}(x,z),\mathsf{V}(x)\rangle,\mathsf{S}(x))\le\varepsilon$
for every $(x,z)\in\mathcal{R}$; it is witness-indistinguishable with error
$\varepsilon$ if that distance between the views of any two valid witnesses of
the same $x$ is at most $\varepsilon$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Reduction to witness-indistinguishability]\label{lem:reduction}
Let $\mathsf{S}(x)$ pick arbitrary valid compressions, build the corresponding
witness $z_0$, and run the honest prover with fresh masks. Then $\mathsf{S}$ is
efficient, receives no witness, and for every valid $z$,
$\Dsd(\mathrm{View}(z),\mathsf{S}(x)) = \Dsd(\mathrm{View}(z),\mathrm{View}(z_0))$.
Hence honest-verifier zero-knowledge with error $\varepsilon$ follows from
full-transcript witness-indistinguishability with error $\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\mathsf{S}$ is the honest prover run on the particular valid witness $z_0$ it
generated, so $\mathsf{S}(x)$ and $\mathrm{View}(z_0)$ are the same
distribution; substitute. Efficiency is the cost of one honest proof. Note the
quantifier: $z_0$ is chosen by $\mathsf{S}$ and may be any valid witness, which
is legitimate precisely because $x$ constrains none of it.
\end{proof}

The consequence worth stating plainly is a limitation. If the statement bound a
hash output, a chaining value, or a message --- as the hash-chain statement does
--- then $\mathsf{S}$ could not generate a witness matching that value without
inverting the hash, and Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} would fail. Nothing in this
paper extends to such statements; the API refuses them.

\paragraph{What a statement with no public input is good for.} On its own,
``there exist $2^n$ valid BLAKE3 compressions'' is not a useful assertion. Its
value is threefold: it is the inner statement of larger protocols that supply
the binding externally; it is the setting in which the masking construction can
be isolated and certified without the simulator question being entangled with a
hash-inversion question; and it is the honest boundary of what the present
evidence supports. Extending the binding is future work, not a corollary.

\section{Protocol overview}\label{sec:overview}

The amended protocol, with the privacy purpose of each step.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Extend the witness with randomizer rows.} Two species, on fresh
witness columns filled with uniform bits: $A$-type rows $u\cdot 1 = u$ and
$B$-type rows $1\cdot u' = u'$. \emph{Purpose:} blind the affine transcript
classes (round-1 vectors, lincheck, ring-switch, PCS values).
\item \textbf{Commit the witness hidingly}, with a low-half mask $\mu$ and a
full-support blinder $g$. \emph{Purpose:} hide the opened codeword rows and
everything downstream of them in the recursion.
\item \textbf{Commit two fresh witness-free multilinears $P,Q$ hidingly.}
\emph{Purpose:} supply a degree-2 mask channel; committing first is what stops
the prover from choosing them after seeing the challenge that batches them.
\item \textbf{Bind $\sigma_z=\sum_x \mathrm{eq}(r,x)P(x)Q(x)$} into the
transcript. \emph{Purpose:} fix the mask channel's contribution before the
batching challenge exists.
\item \textbf{Derive $\gamma$} by Fiat--Shamir, after the commitments and
$\sigma_z$. \emph{Purpose:} soundness of the batching (Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}).
\item \textbf{Run the masked zerocheck} on $\hat a\hat b + \gamma PQ$.
\emph{Purpose:} the round messages, the one class no degree-1 mask reaches, are
now covered by a witness-free channel.
\item \textbf{Run lincheck and the ring switch.} \emph{Purpose:} none of their
own --- these classes are affine and already covered by step~1.
\item \textbf{Open the witness and $P,Q$ hidingly at $\rho$, then check
$\hat a(\rho)\hat b(\rho)+\gamma P(\rho)Q(\rho)$.} \emph{Purpose:} the hiding
openings keep the leakage down to the evaluations themselves; a plain opening
would additionally reveal opened rows of $P$, enlarging the conditioning set
(\S\ref{sec:channel}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The transcript splits into an affine part and one hard class}\label{sec:split}

Fix the verifier challenges. Every revealed value except the zerocheck round
messages is affine in the masks with a witness-independent linear part: the
round-1 vectors are protected by 128-bit region alignment, and the lincheck,
ring-switch and PCS values are $\Fbig$-linear in the committed data. For an
affine map, image coverage of the witness-difference directions \emph{is}
distribution equality --- so those classes are hidden exactly when the mask
image covers those directions, which is what the certificates of
\S\ref{sec:certificates} check.

\begin{theorem}[Masking; Lean~4, no \texttt{sorry}]\label{thm:mask}
Let $A:U\to V$ be additive with $U$ finite, $f:W\to V$, and suppose
$f(w)-f(w')\in\Img A$ for the witness pairs of interest. Then for every value
the fiber counts under $w$ and $w'$ are equal, the transcript is uniform on its
coset with multiplicity $|\ker A|$, and $u\mapsto Au+r$ from any public
representative $r$ reproduces the honest distribution exactly. If $A$ is
surjective the coset condition is vacuous.
\end{theorem}

The hypothesis is stated for ``the witness pairs of interest'' because
\S\ref{sec:relation} has no public inputs to condition on: the pairs that matter
are those inducing the same accepted claim value, which the opening genuinely
determines from revealed data. That conditioning is part of the definition of
the view, not a weakening of it.

The exception is real. A zerocheck round sends $(G(1),G(\infty))$ where $G$ is
degree~2 in the fold variable and $G(\infty)$ is its leading coefficient. The
randomizer species enter $\hat a$ and $\hat b$ separately, so $G$ carries genuine
$u_A\!\cdot\!u_B$ cross terms once a fold group spans the region boundary: the
round messages are \emph{not} affine in the masks, and image coverage does not
apply to them. A degree-1 mask cannot reach $G(\infty)$ at all, and in
characteristic~2 the classical separable mask $\sum_i g_i(x_i)$ sums to zero over
the cube. This is the sole obstruction, and \S\ref{sec:channel} is the response
to it.

\section{The degree-2 channel and conditional coverage}\label{sec:channel}

Run the zerocheck on $\hat a\hat b + \gamma PQ$ with $P,Q$ fresh, witness-free,
full-support multilinears committed before $\gamma$ exists. Each round message
becomes $G_j+\gamma M_j$ and the final check gains $\gamma P(\rho)Q(\rho)$.
Because $P,Q$ carry no witness, the mask contribution's distribution is
witness-independent by construction. What must be checked is that it is
\emph{jointly} sufficient, and the joint condition is strictly stronger than the
marginal one.

\paragraph{A two-dimensional example.} Let the transcript be
$T(w,p)=(w_1+p_1,\;w_2+p_2)$ over $\Ftwo$ with uniform mask $p$, and suppose the
protocol also reveals $\ell(p)=p_1+p_2$. Marginally each coordinate of $T$ is
uniform and reveals nothing. Jointly, $T_1+T_2+\ell = w_1+w_2$: a witness
functional in the clear. The mask image is all of $\Ftwo^2$, and that is not
enough. The right condition restricts the mask to the kernel of the revealed
functional: $d\in R(\ker \ell)$, where here $\ker\ell=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}$ and the
reachable set is only the diagonal, which is exactly the set of witness
differences that stay hidden.

\paragraph{The real condition.} The same $P$ that masks the round messages is
revealed through $P(\rho)$, through $\sigma_z$, and through every value inside
$P$'s own opening. Writing $L$ for the map collecting all of them, the condition
is
\[
  d \;\in\; R(\ker L) \;+\; \Img(\text{outer stage}) \;+\; \langle \text{claim direction}\rangle
  \qquad\text{for every witness difference } d .
\]
Three things about this deserve emphasis. First, it is why $P,Q$ must be opened
\emph{hidingly}: a plain opening would put the opened rows of $P$ into $L$,
shrinking $\ker L$ and un-covering claim-preserving directions. Second, the
direction the inner stage cannot reach is not ``public''; it is an internal claim
direction that the affine channel hides and that the outer stage carries --- see
below. Third, the certificate must be the joint conditional form over the whole
$P$-dependent transcript, not a per-coordinate check.

\paragraph{Why the composition is triangular, not a sum of images.} An earlier
version of this argument composed the randomizer channel and the $P\cdot Q$
channel by adding their images. That step is invalid: adding images requires both
channels to be additive maps on the transcript space, and the randomizer channel
is certifiably \emph{bilinear} on exactly the round-pair coordinates it was
needed for. The valid structure is triangular. Fix the outer masks $(u_B,Q)$; the
transcript is then affine in the inner masks $(u_A,\mu,g,P)$. Quotient by the
inner image and require the residual to be affine in the outer masks with a
witness-independent linear part, with claim-equal witnesses' residues differing
inside the outer image.

\begin{theorem}[Triangular composition; Lean~4, no \texttt{sorry}]\label{thm:tri}
Let $T(w,u,b)=A_{w,b}u+g_{w,b}$ with $U$, $B$ finite. If
(H1) $\Img A_{w,b}=V_0$ for all $w,b$;
(H2) $\pi(g_{w,b})=\mathcal{B}(b)+d(w)$ in $V/V_0$ for an additive
$\mathcal{B}$ independent of $w$; and
(H3) $d(w)-d(w')\in\Img\mathcal{B}$ for claim-equal $w,w'$;
then the joint distribution of $T$ over uniform $(u,b)$ is identical for $w$ and
$w'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Mixture with a bad set; Lean~4, no \texttt{sorry}]\label{thm:badset}
If the per-$b$ distributions agree for every $b$ outside a set $\mathrm{Bad}$,
then the mixtures over uniform $b$ are at statistical distance at most
$|\mathrm{Bad}|/|B|$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:badset} is where $\erank$ formally lives: $\mathrm{Bad}$ is the
set of mask draws for which the coverage above fails.

\section{Certificates, certified configurations, and $\erank$}\label{sec:certificates}

An \emph{exact $\Ftwo$ image-coverage certificate} extracts the true linear image
of a mask channel by unit-probing every mask coordinate of the real prover and
then decides membership by Gaussian elimination. It is exact in the image
extraction; the witness-difference directions it tests are a structured
spanning family plus random draws, and the challenge tuple is fixed per run.
The PCS layer is additionally supported by two structural lemmas rather than by
probing alone --- the low-half mask occupies the low degree-graded basis slots,
so its map to opened symbols is surjective at every query set identically in the
challenges, and every downstream value is $\Fbig$-linear in the blinded message.

\paragraph{What the certificates establish.} All numbers below are measured on
the real amended prover at the reduced-size fixture, whose complete algebraic
transcript is 1061 field elements (586 witness-dependent, 474 mask-only).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1 holds, and the degree-2 channel is what makes it hold.} The
inner-mask image on the round-pair block spans the \emph{same} 2432-bit subspace
at two different witnesses when the $P$ channel is active. With the channel
disabled the two spans differ. So the constant-image hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tri} is not vacuous, and it is the degree-2 channel that
discharges it.
\item \textbf{The inner stage falls exactly one direction short, and it is the
expected one.} The inner image covers 2432 of the block's 2560 bits; the missing
128 bits are exactly the $\hat b$-side final evaluation, whose inner-mask
derivative is identically zero. This is the coordinate that disproved an earlier
flat-mixture argument. The outer ($u_B$) stage supplies it: the joint image is
2560 of 2560 bits.
\item \textbf{No claim-preserving witness direction escapes, on the round-pair
class.} Conditioned on the complete mask-only leakage set and on \emph{every}
public claim the verifier learns --- the ab-claim, the reduced evaluation
$v=\hat z(r)$ that the opening proves, and the $\hat c$ claim, a 384-bit claim
space that the witness-difference family saturates --- the residual of every
tested difference modulo the joint image is determined by the claim.
\item \textbf{The complete transcript is covered.} Over all 548
witness-dependent coordinates of the complete A1$'$ transcript, at three
challenge tuples, with the complete mask-only leakage set and every public
claim conditioned on and the claim space saturated,
$\mathrm{rank}[\text{residual}\mid\Delta\text{claim}]
= \mathrm{rank}(\Delta\text{claim})$: no claim-preserving witness direction
escapes the joint image.
\item \textbf{Four corrections were needed to get an interpretable verdict},
and each had made earlier results uninterpretable rather than wrong in the
safe direction. The criterion is capped by the number of sampled witness
directions, so below the claim dimension it passes for free. The witness
family must be genuinely linear. The PCS masks are \emph{field}-valued, so
probing one bit per slot samples $1/128$ of that channel --- representing its
image as an $\Fbig$-subspace instead is also what makes the certificate
affordable. And $\shatv$ binds the randomizer sizing: slice $r$ sees only
witness bits at positions $\equiv r \pmod{128}$, so the requirement is
blocks $\times$ chunks $\ge 128$ \emph{with margin}; the residual
concentrated in exactly that class at $1.5\times$ margin and vanished at
$3\times$. Production BLAKE3 runs at $\sim\!12\times$.
\item \textbf{The bad set is nonempty and delimited.} At a constant $Q$ the
channel's image collapses from 2305 to 1153 bits --- it reaches the $G(1)$ half
but not the degree-2 $G(\infty)$ half, which is precisely its reason for
existing.
\item \textbf{The detector is not vacuous.} Disabling the PCS mask, or appending
an unmasked witness functional to the transcript vector, both break the
certificate.
\item \textbf{Measured at the production configuration, not assumed.} At
$m=22$ the degree-2 channel spans the round-pair block in full (4096 of 4096
bits, by random-probe spanning through the real fold kernels, which can only
under-estimate a rank); a degenerate $Q$ spans exactly half, so the
measurement is not vacuous at that size either; the per-proof coverage
self-check passes with no resampling and its proof verifies; and the
randomizer margin that binds the $\shatv$ slices is $12\times$ the
requirement there, against $3\times$ at the certified fixture.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A vacuity failure mode, and the guard against it.} The coverage
criterion compares the rank of $[\text{residual}\mid\Delta\text{claim}]$ with the
rank of $\Delta\text{claim}$. Both are capped by the number of sampled witness
directions, so with fewer than $128$ directions --- the claim's bit dimension ---
the equality can hold merely because no claim-preserving combination exists among
the samples. An earlier certificate in this line of work used $40$ and is vacuous
by this measure. The certificates here assert claim saturation before any verdict
is recorded.

\paragraph{$\erank$, honestly.} A previous version of this argument bounded
$\erank$ by Schwartz--Zippel over ``uniform mask entries in $\Fbig$''. That bound
is withdrawn. The implemented $P,Q$ are \emph{Boolean} --- one bit per cube entry
--- so the entries range over $\{0,1\}$, where Schwartz--Zippel at these degrees
is vacuous; the bound also conflated a single entry's degree with a
$2304\times2304$ determinant's, and took the probability over a different
variable set from the one the degree was counted in. What replaces it is a
per-proof check: coverage of the round-pair block conditioned on the mask-only
leakage is an $\Ftwo$ rank condition on data the prover already has, so the
prover verifies it for its own draw and resamples $P,Q$ on failure. The failure
event depends only on the mask draw and the challenges, both witness-independent,
so resampling leaks nothing. Consequently $\erank = 0$ for every emitted proof,
and what remains unquantified is the resample probability --- a liveness
quantity, not a privacy one. A closed-form bound over Boolean masks remains open.
(Had $P,Q$ been drawn from $\Fbig$ instead, a corrected Schwartz--Zippel argument
would apply and give roughly $2^{-109}$ at production size; that variant is not
what is implemented.)

\section{Soundness of the amendment}\label{sec:soundness}

Privacy and soundness are argued separately here, and neither is used to justify
the other.

\begin{lemma}[$\gamma$-batching]\label{lem:gamma}
Completeness: for an honest prover both claims telescope through the same round
schedule, so the final check holds identically. Soundness: with all prover
messages fixed, the verifier's final residual is affine in $\sigma_z$ with slope
$\gamma\prod_j(1+\rho_j)/(1+r_{\mathrm{eq},j})$; writing $\delta$ and $\delta'$
for the two sides' defects, acceptance requires $\delta+\gamma\delta'=0$. For
$(\delta,\delta')\ne 0$ at most one $\gamma$ satisfies this, so a prover who must
fix the defects before seeing $\gamma$ succeeds with probability at most
$2^{-128}$, on top of the unchanged sumcheck error.
\end{lemma}

The ordering supplies the ``before'': the $P,Q$ commitments and $\sigma_z$ are
absorbed into the transcript before $\gamma$ is sampled (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
This is checked constructively rather than asserted: on an invalid witness we
measure the affine response, solve for the unique $\sigma_z$ that cancels the
defect, and observe that the patched proof is accepted when the challenge is
fixed in advance --- the ordering attack is real --- and rejected under
Fiat--Shamir and at a hundred further challenge tuples. Knowledge soundness is
unchanged: mask columns satisfy no real constraint, so every accepting extended
witness restricts to a valid original one, and the extractor runs on the
real-wire sub-witness unmodified.

\section{Assumptions, Fiat--Shamir, and the leakage boundary}\label{sec:assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Sibling hiding]
Model the leaf hash as a random oracle, or assume it is pseudorandom on
high-min-entropy inputs. Each level-0 leaf carries its 64 blinder lane symbols,
i.e.\ $8192$ bits of fresh randomness; conditioning on all revealed data leaves
each unopened leaf with min-entropy $\kmin\ge 8192-512$ bits, so an unopened
sibling hash is distinguishable from uniform with advantage at most
$\ehash \le q\cdot 2^{-\kmin}$ over $q$ queries.
\end{assumption}

This is stated, not derived from collision resistance; collision resistance gives
binding, not hiding.

The interactive argument is public-coin and the simulator of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} is non-programming, so the Fiat--Shamir lift needs no
oracle programming and no rewinding: the same $\mathsf{S}$ is the NIZK simulator
in the classical ROM with the same error. Proof-of-work grinding and challenge
derivation are ordinary parts of the transcript $\mathsf{S}$ itself produces.

The transcript is allowed to reveal, and reveals only: the statement digest, the
batch size, proof size and parameter selection, the Fiat--Shamir challenges,
proof-of-work nonces, query positions, and the accepted claim as a constrained
quantity. Everything else is either covered by a mask image or computationally
hidden. Timing, retry counts, memory access patterns and error messages are side
channels and are out of scope.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The randomizer rows are the ethSTARK randomizer-trace idea transplanted to a
batch-R1CS PIOP~\cite{ethSTARK}; the hiding opening follows Ligero/Ligerito-style
masking~\cite{Ligerito}; the degree-2 $P\cdot Q$ channel is the
Chiesa--Forbes--Spooner masking-polynomial idea~\cite{CFS17} rebuilt to survive
characteristic~2, where the classical separable mask vanishes over the cube.
Diamond~\cite{Diamond25} gives a zero-knowledge polynomial commitment over binary
fields; whether it could replace the hiding opening --- removing $\mu$ and $g$
--- is open and worth settling. What is new here is the reduction of
\S\ref{sec:relation}, the triangular composition of \S\ref{sec:channel}, and the
exact conditional image-coverage methodology of \S\ref{sec:certificates}.
Flock itself is described in~\cite{Flock}; the ring-switching front end follows
\cite{DP24}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Unsupported statement families.} SHA-256, Keccak, hash chains, and
Merkle-path statements have no zero-knowledge mode. Chains are excluded on
principle: they bind public endpoints, which breaks Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}.
\item \textbf{Unsupported parameters.} Only the configurations of
Table~\ref{tab:configs}. Others are refused by the API rather than proved.
\item \textbf{$\erank$.} Zero per emitted proof by construction (the per-proof
check), but the resample probability has no closed-form bound over Boolean masks.
\item \textbf{The certificate passes on the synthetic vehicle but not yet on
a real BLAKE3 statement, and the gap is now a specific measured direction.}
The criterion is usable on the hash statement family --- a \emph{pass} is
sound even without a linear witness sub-family, since every genuine
claim-equal pair is among the combinations it quantifies over --- so this was
run directly on a real BLAKE3 batch statement ($m=20$, 64 blocks, the same
$3\times$ randomizer margin the fixture certificate needed), restricted to the
PIOP coordinate classes so the image could be saturated. The triangular
structure reproduces: the inner stage spans 21{,}504 of 30{,}464 bits and the
outer species adds exactly 128 more, the single direction the fixture showed
it is responsible for. But with the claim space saturated at 384 bits,
$\mathrm{rank}[\text{residual}\mid\Delta\text{claim}] = 512$: \emph{one}
$\Fbig$ direction of claim-preserving witness difference is unaccounted for.
That is a lead, not a proven leak --- the codomain is restricted, probing is
random rather than exhaustive, and the criterion's failure direction is the
conservative one. It is also \emph{localized}: residual attribution places it
on the C-side and lincheck coordinates (\texttt{round1\_c}, the lincheck
rounds, \texttt{z\_partial}) and not on the round pairs or final evaluations,
which the degree-2 channel and the outer stage do cover. Since $c_{\mathrm{eval}}$
--- one of the three conditioned claims --- is itself an interpolation of
\texttt{round1\_c}, a single conditioned scalar sits on a 64-element class
there, and determining what else the verifier learns about that class is the
concrete next step. Until it is resolved, the whole-transcript claim rests on
the fixture certificate plus structural transfer, and this is the principal
open item.
\item \textbf{Certificate scope.} Certificates are computed at the reduced
fixture; transfer to production size rests on structural identity of the maps,
not on a production-size full-support probe.
\item \textbf{Challenge tuples.} Certificates are computed at sampled fixed
tuples, including tuples replayed from real Fiat--Shamir transcripts. Coverage
for all tuples is not established.
\item \textbf{Optimized-prover equivalence.} Not established. The optimized
prover runs an un-amended zerocheck and is outside the claim; making it
equivalent is a differential-testing task, not a proof.
\item \textbf{ROM assumption.} Sibling hiding is assumed, not derived.
\item \textbf{Independent review.} None has been performed. The review dossier
accompanying this note is the intended entry point.
\item \textbf{Side channels and QROM.} Out of scope; no claims.
\end{itemize}
