\begin{abstract}
The original Flock paper and implementation provide succinct proofs for
batched BLAKE3 computation, but do not provide zero knowledge: the proof
transcript contains deterministic functions of the witness \cite{Flock}.  This
paper develops Flock-ZK for the same BLAKE3 circuit.  BLAKE3 is the first target
because it is Flock's original benchmark and therefore gives a direct comparison
between the ZK and non-ZK provers.  A companion implementation provides a Rust
prover, verifier, simulator, and extractor, together with Lean lemmas and
replayable evidence.

The implementation registers two statements over the same batch of 256
single-block circuit instances.  The batch statement preserves Flock's benchmark
shape but does not bind the trace to public hash outputs.  The fixed-digest
statement proves the relation
\[
  y_i=\operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i),
  \qquad |x_i|=64\text{ bytes},
  \qquad |y_i|=32\text{ bytes},
\]
for 256 public digests while hiding the messages.  Honest and simulated proofs
have the same format and are accepted by the same Flock-ZK verifier.  There is
no simulator-only verification rule; this is not the original non-ZK verifier.

The proof has two algebraic steps.  First, a field-valued mask makes the PIOP
messages independent of the witness after conditioning on the two mask values
that are later opened.  For the registered shape, the relevant determinant has
degree at most 720, as derived in Section~\ref{sec:s2}.  Second, an affine
translation of the PCS blinders preserves every opened value and every
recursive codeword.  These two translations reduce simulation to choosing the
Fiat--Shamir challenges first, solving the dependent messages, and programming
18 fresh random-oracle points: one point for $z$, one for $\gamma$, and sixteen
fold challenges.  The simulator receives only the public statement.

In the classical programmable random-oracle model, the explicit algebraic and
programming terms sum to less than $2^{-118.502}$ at a hash-query budget
$Q_H=2^{64}$, plus a separately stated oracle-boundary term.  Thus the two
registered profiles are computationally zero knowledge under the assumptions
stated here.  Knowledge security is separate: one L0 term caps the available
standalone Fiat--Shamir reduction over $\F_{2^{128}}$ at 55.994 bits for the
same query budget, before other knowledge-error terms are added.  The stronger
100-bit deployment target is conjectural, not a theorem of this paper.  At the
registered batch size of 256, the current unoptimized ZK
prover is 6.78 times slower than the current non-ZK prover on twelve threads,
and its encoded proof is 9.21 times larger.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and scope}\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Result in one sentence.}
For the two parameter sets in Table~\ref{tab:profiles}, a proof generated from
a valid witness is computationally indistinguishable from a proof generated
from the public statement alone, in the classical programmable-random-oracle
model.

Flock-ZK starts with BLAKE3 for scope, not because the masking method is
incompatible with other hashes.  A zero-knowledge theorem attaches to an exact
relation, circuit layout, transcript, and PCS parameter set.  BLAKE3 already had
Flock's most mature circuit and non-ZK benchmark, so it was the smallest target
that permits both a complete implementation and a meaningful performance
comparison.

The fixed-digest relation is
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\rm fd}
  =\left\{
    \bigl((y_1,\ldots,y_{256}),(x_1,\ldots,x_{256})\bigr):
    y_i=\operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i),\ |x_i|=64\text{ bytes},
    \ |y_i|=32\text{ bytes}
  \right\}.
\]
The batch relation uses the same BLAKE3 circuit but has no externally fixed
digest list or hash output.  Its statement therefore exposes only the batch
shape; the prover is free to choose the batch trace.  The fixed-digest profile
is the stronger application statement: it proves knowledge of preimages for
public digests while hiding those preimages.  In both cases the R1CS shape,
packing convention, PCS parameters, and circuit digest are part of the
registered statement family.

Throughout the paper, \emph{computational zero knowledge} has its standard
classical-ROM meaning: for every classical probabilistic polynomial-time
distinguisher making at most $Q_H$ oracle queries, the joint view of an honest
proof and its oracle answers is computationally indistinguishable from the
view produced by a simulator that knows only the statement.  The simulator may
program points that the distinguisher has not already queried.  This is the IOP
and Fiat--Shamir setting of Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, and Spooner
\cite{BCS}; the algebraic masking step is in the spirit of zero-knowledge
sumcheck \cite{CFS17}.

The scope is deliberately narrow.  The preimage circuit hashes one message of
exactly 64 bytes and pins the BLAKE3 IV, counter, block length, and
\texttt{CHUNK\_START}, \texttt{CHUNK\_END}, and \texttt{ROOT} flags.  Unknown
circuit digests, batch sizes, and PCS parameters are rejected before proving.
SHA-256 statements, Keccak statements, longer BLAKE3 messages, hash chains,
Merkle-path statements, recursion, side channels, and quantum random-oracle
queries are not covered.  This is an absence of a family-specific proof, not a
claim that the construction cannot be adapted.  Section~\ref{sec:extension}
lists the obligations that must be re-established for each new family.

\paragraph{Construction at a glance.}
The argument follows the data seen by the verifier:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the BLAKE3 computation is written as an R1CS assignment $z$ satisfying
      $(Az)\circ(Bz)=Cz$;
\item zerocheck reduces all multiplication constraints to evaluations at a
      transcript-derived point;
\item lincheck ties those evaluations back to the public matrices $A,B,C$ and
      the same committed assignment $z$;
\item ring switching converts the bit-level evaluation claims into packed
      extension-field claims, which Ligerito commits to and opens;
\item independent masks randomize every witness-dependent value while
      preserving the values that the verifier checks; and
\item the simulator samples those randomized values from the public statement
      and programs only fresh, domain-separated oracle points.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~\ref{sec:protocol}--\ref{sec:simulation} develop this construction in
order.  Section~\ref{sec:performance} measures its cost.  Sections
\ref{sec:extract} and \ref{sec:knowledge} treat extraction separately because
witness privacy and knowledge soundness are different properties.  Section
\ref{sec:evidence} states exactly which obligations are proved in Lean, checked
by executable certificates, or left as explicit assumptions.

\begin{assumption}[Classical programmable random oracle]\label{ass:rom}
All injectively framed SHA-256 calls are modelled as one random function
$\RO:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{256}$.  The adversary makes classical queries, and
the simulator may program a point only before that point is queried.  No QROM
claim is made.
\end{assumption}

\section{Relation and construction}\label{sec:protocol}

Let $z$ be the packed binary assignment for the block-diagonal BLAKE3 R1CS,
embedded in $\K$, and write
\[
  a=Az,\qquad b=Bz,\qquad c_z=Cz.
\]
The R1CS is satisfied when $a\circ b=c_z$.  Zerocheck treats the vectors as
multilinear polynomials and reduces the assertion
$\widehat a(y)\widehat b(y)+\widehat c_z(y)=0$ on the Boolean cube to terminal
evaluations at a random point.  Each round folds one or more variables and
leaves a smaller claim.  A false residual polynomial reaches the terminal
identity only on a small exceptional set of challenges.

Zerocheck alone does not show that the prover used $a=Az$, $b=Bz$, and
$c_z=Cz$.  Lincheck supplies that link.  It randomly combines the public
matrix relations, asks for a partial evaluation of $z$, and reduces consistency
to evaluation claims on the same committed assignment.  Thus zerocheck checks
the multiplicative constraints, while lincheck prevents the prover from
inventing unrelated vectors that merely satisfy their product equation.

The resulting claims are opened with the ring-switched Ligerito PCS
\cite{Ligerito,DP24}.  The companion cheat sheet gives a high-level account of
Ligerito, its relationship to Ligero, and ring switching.

For the fixed-digest family, the verifier derives an additional packed opening
claim from the public digest list.  Before the first challenge, the transcript
binds that list, the padding rule, the witness layout, the circuit matrices, the
parameter set, and a fresh 32-byte proof nonce.

The ZK prover adds four independent mask sources: the zerocheck mask $P$, the
lincheck mask $S$, and the round-one masks $S_c,S_h$.  The witness and mask
commitments use independent hiding randomness, denoted $\mu$ and $g$.  Every
stream, including the proof nonce, is derived from a per-proof DRBG under a
distinct label.

The main zerocheck mask is
\[
  \gamma P(X)Q^\star(X),
  \qquad
  Q^\star(X)=q_0+\sum_j q_jX_j.
\]
Here $P$ is a fresh witness-free polynomial, committed before the verifier
samples $\gamma$.  Its coefficients are uniform in $\K$ on a translated
subcube with $d_{\log}=12$ variables.  The polynomial $Q^\star$ is fixed,
public, affine, and nonzero on the Boolean cube.  Multiplication by $Q^\star$
gives a degree-two mask with enough independent directions to randomize both
coordinates of each degree-two zerocheck round message; Proposition
\ref{prop:coverage} makes that statement quantitative.  The challenge
$\gamma$ prevents the prover from choosing $P$ after seeing how it would need
to cancel an invalid computation.  At the terminal point $\rho$, the verifier
checks
\[
  \widehat a(\rho)\widehat b(\rho)
  +\gamma P(\rho)Q^\star(\rho),
\]
and authenticates $P(\rho)$ against its earlier commitment.  The mask therefore
changes the visible proof distribution without becoming a free correction to
the relation.  The separate masks $S_c,S_h$ cover the first zerocheck message,
and $S$ covers lincheck.

\section{One random-oracle boundary}\label{sec:ro}

In Flock-ZK, Fiat--Shamir challenges, Merkle commitments, and proof of work are
all implemented through the one random oracle of Assumption~\ref{ass:rom}, but
domain separation prevents them from using the same input.  Each input begins
with a role byte.  A Merkle input also binds
the wire-protocol identifier, proof nonce, commitment channel, recursion depth, node
level, node index, payload length, and payload.  A proof-of-work input is
\[
  \texttt{ROLE\_POW}\parallel\textit{state}\parallel\textit{nonce}.
\]
The proof nonce is absorbed immediately after the statement and before the
first challenge.

This framing is part of the proof, not merely an encoding convention.  It lets
the extractor identify witness-leaf queries without relying on their order,
prevents simulator programming from aliasing transcript or Merkle inputs, and
makes proofs from different nonces or commitment channels use different
oracle points.  Native, recording, and programming backends implement the same
frames.  When no point is programmed they return identical bytes.  Scalar and
four-way SIMD implementations are differentially tested against the one-shot
definition for every supported tail shape.

\section{The PIOP translation}\label{sec:s2}

Fix the post-commitment challenge tuple $\chi$.  Let $D$ be the coefficient
space of $P$.  If $n=m-6$ is the number of masked zerocheck rounds, define
\[
  R_\chi:D\longrightarrow\K^{2n}
\]
to be the map from mask coefficients to the $2n$ round-message coordinates.
Let
\[
  L_\chi:D\longrightarrow\K^2
\]
map the same coefficients to the two mask values revealed later: the initial
masked claim and $P(\rho)$.  The kernel $\ker L_\chi$ is simply the set of
masks that change neither revealed value.  Once $L_\chi(P)$ is fixed, these are
the only mask changes still available.  Their effect on the round messages is
the subspace $R_\chi(\ker L_\chi)$.

\begin{lemma}[Conditioned image dimension]\label{lem:conditioned-image}
Let $N=\dim D$.  If
\[
  \rank\!\begin{bmatrix}R_\chi\\L_\chi\end{bmatrix}=2n,
  \qquad
  \rank L_\chi=2,
\]
then
\[
  \dim R_\chi(\ker L_\chi)=2n-2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The stacked-rank assumption gives
$\dim(\ker R_\chi\cap\ker L_\chi)=N-2n$, while $\rank L_\chi=2$ gives
$\dim\ker L_\chi=N-2$.  Rank--nullity for $R_\chi$ restricted to
$\ker L_\chi$ now gives
\[
  \dim R_\chi(\ker L_\chi)
  =(N-2)-(N-2n)=2n-2.
\]
\end{proof}

For the registered parameters, $m=22$ and $n=16$, so $R_\chi$ has 32 output
coordinates.  The sumcheck recurrence makes the two rows of $L_\chi$
dependent on the rows of $R_\chi$, so the stacked map has rank at most 32.  To
prove that it has rank 32, it is enough to find a square $32\times32$
submatrix, called a \emph{minor}, with nonzero determinant.

The symbolic evaluator builds the entries of this matrix as polynomials in the
32 verifier challenges.  It follows the same straight-line recurrences as the
concrete evaluator, while tracking either field values or conservative
multivariate degrees.  It contains no challenge-dependent inversion.  The
selected minor evaluates to a nonzero field element at one recorded challenge
tuple, proving that its determinant is not the zero polynomial.  Summing the
per-row degree bounds gives total degree at most 720.  Thus 720 is not a
constraint count or a fitted constant; it is a conservative degree bound for
this determinant.

\begin{proposition}[Registered field-mask coverage]\label{prop:coverage}
For uniformly sampled $\chi\in\K^{32}$, the registered Flock-ZK shape satisfies
the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:conditioned-image}, except with probability
\[
  \Pr_\chi[\text{coverage failure}]
  \le \frac{720}{2^{128}}
  <2^{-118.508}.
\]
Consequently, after the two revealed mask values are fixed, the remaining
mask freedom covers all $2n-2=30$ independent round-message directions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The nonzero minor has total degree at most 720.  Schwartz--Zippel bounds the
probability that it vanishes at a uniform challenge tuple by
$720/|\K|=720/2^{128}$ \cite{Schwartz,Zippel}.  Outside that zero set, the
minor gives rank 32 for the stacked map, the separately checked leakage map has
rank 2, and Lemma~\ref{lem:conditioned-image} applies.  Section
\ref{sec:evidence} records how the matrix, nonzero evaluation, and degree
vector are replayed.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage} proves the PIOP masking step.  It says nothing by
itself about privacy at the PCS opening boundary; that is the next step.

\section{The PCS translation}\label{sec:s3}

Write the initial combined opening vector as
\[
  F=(\mu\parallel z)+c\,g,
\]
where $c\in\K$ is sampled after commitment.  Let $d$ be a witness difference
that preserves every public opening claim.  The aim is to replace $z$ by
$z+d$ without changing anything the verifier opens.

\begin{lemma}[Opening-preserving translation]\label{lem:pcs}
Suppose $c\ne0$ and the initial-level query system has full row rank.  There is
an affine bijection on the uniform PCS masks such that replacing $z$ by $z+d$
leaves the queried initial rows and the entire combined vector $F$ unchanged.
Consequently every recursive codeword, root, authentication path, sumcheck
value, and out-of-domain sample is unchanged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
  \delta g_{\rm top}=c^{-1}d.
\]
Choose $\delta\mu$ to solve the queried initial-level encoding equations, and
set
\[
  \delta g_{\rm lo}=c^{-1}\delta\mu.
\]
Because the field has characteristic two,
$d+c\,\delta g_{\rm top}=0$ and
$\delta\mu+c\,\delta g_{\rm lo}=0$.  Thus $\delta F=0$ globally.  The defining
equations for $\delta\mu$ also hold the separately opened initial rows fixed.
Translation by these deltas is a bijection on uniform masks, so it preserves
their distribution.
\end{proof}

The registered verifier opens 218 initial-level positions.  Each encoded lane
starts with 512 independent mask symbols, so the query system is strictly
underdetermined.  There are 128 wide lanes, and the conservative rank audit
leaves at least one independent element of $\K$ per lane in every relevant
fresh leaf.  Hence the minimum conditional entropy used at the Merkle boundary
is $128\cdot128=16{,}384$ bits.  No recursive entropy charge is needed because
Lemma~\ref{lem:pcs} makes the recursive codewords identical.  The exceptional
event $c=0$ has probability $2^{-128}$.  Section~\ref{sec:evidence} describes
the executable query-rank and opening-field checks.

\section{Simulator and concrete ZK bound}\label{sec:simulation}

Once Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage} and Lemma~\ref{lem:pcs} are separated, the
simulator is direct.  It receives a public-statement capability containing the
setup and digest list, but no witness type.  It constructs an unrelated trace,
overwrites the public-output region, samples the terminal zerocheck values, and
solves the dependent messages in one pass.  The patched trace is not an R1CS
witness: running the honest prover on it is rejected.  After the simulator
programs the required fresh oracle points, however, the Flock-ZK verifier
accepts the simulated proof.

For $m=22$, zerocheck has $m-6=16$ multilinear fold rounds.  The simulator
programs one oracle point for the univariate fold challenge $z$, one for the
masking challenge $\gamma$, and one for each of the sixteen fold challenges
$\rho_j$.  This accounts for the bound of 18 programmed points.

A hybrid argument compares two distributions through intermediate thought
experiments.  Each row of Table~\ref{tab:games} replaces one part of the honest
proof by its simulated counterpart.  The charge bounds the additional
distinguishing advantage introduced by that replacement.  Adding the charges
bounds the distance between the first and last hybrids.

The three algebraic numerators are $720$, $1$, and $2$: the symbolic bad set,
$c=0$, and the two rejected values of the terminal challenge.  Thus the part
of the bound tracked explicitly by the executable ledger is
\[
  \varepsilon_{\rm explicit}(Q_H)
  =
  \frac{723}{2^{128}}
  +\frac{18Q_H}{2^{256}}
  +\frac{Q_H}{2^{16384}}.
\]
At $Q_H=2^{64}$,
\[
  -\log_2\varepsilon_{\rm explicit}(2^{64})
  =118.502\ldots.
\]
The term $\varepsilon_{\rm RO}$ denotes collision and binding error at the
framed 256-bit oracle boundary.  It remains symbolic because a full count must
include both adversarial queries and the protocol's own Merkle, transcript, and
grinding queries.  It is not included in the quoted 118.502-bit figure, so that
number is the explicit algebraic and programming ledger, not a complete
standalone security level.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped computational zero knowledge]\label{thm:zk}
Assume the classical programmable random oracle of Assumption \ref{ass:rom},
the conditioned coverage of Proposition \ref{prop:coverage}, the opening
translation of Lemma \ref{lem:pcs}, and freshness of the programmed points.
Then both profiles in Table \ref{tab:profiles} are computationally zero
knowledge.  For every classical distinguisher making at most $Q_H$ oracle
queries,
\[
  \Adv_{\rm ZK}
  \le \varepsilon_{\rm explicit}(Q_H)+\varepsilon_{\rm RO}.
\]
The simulator uses only the public statement, and the verifier is the same
Flock-ZK verifier used for honest proofs.  The original non-ZK Flock verifier
is a different protocol path and is not meant to accept these proofs.
\end{theorem}

This theorem is in the classical programmable-ROM.  It is not a QROM theorem,
and it does not turn the ideal-oracle argument into a standard-model theorem
for SHA-256.

\section{Extraction and simulation extractability}\label{sec:extract}

Zero knowledge means that a proof reveals nothing about the witness beyond the
truth of the statement.  Knowledge soundness asks the complementary question:
can a successful prover be used to recover a witness?  The two properties use
different arguments and have different concrete bounds.

The recording oracle observes the framed leaf queries used to construct the
witness commitment.  Given a complete valid codeword, the extractor rebuilds
the interleaved word, applies the inverse NTT lane by lane, re-encodes the
candidate, checks the configured unique-radius condition, and returns the
witness half.  The system-specific step then reads the 512 message bits of
each instance and independently verifies
\[
  \operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i)=y_i.
\]
Honest recorded commitments yield the true preimages; simulated commitments
do not extract.

The executable decoder is an exact-word decoder and candidate validator.  It
is not a complete Reed--Solomon decoder for every noisy word inside the
theoretical radius.  The general noisy-word step therefore comes from the
Ligerito proximity and unique-decoding argument, not from the current decoder
routine.

For simulation extractability, the simulator records
\[
  (\textit{statement digest},\textit{proof nonce},\textit{protocol identifier})
\]
for each simulated proof.  Extraction refuses every recorded prefix and is
attempted only on a fresh one.  The implemented property is therefore
\emph{fresh-prefix weak simulation extractability}.  Same-prefix simulation
extractability is not claimed.

\section{Knowledge-security limit}\label{sec:knowledge}

The knowledge ledger contains an interactive L0 fold term
\[
  \kappa_\Gamma=\frac{257}{2^{128}},
\]
or 119.994 bits.  The classical Fiat--Shamir reduction multiplies this term by
$Q_H+1$.  At $Q_H=2^{64}$ this term alone becomes
\[
  -\log_2\bigl((Q_H+1)\kappa_\Gamma\bigr)
  =55.994\ldots\text{ bits}.
\]
Thus 55.994 bits is a ceiling imposed by one term, not a completed lower
guarantee for the whole argument.  Every additional positive knowledge-error
term can only reduce the final number of provable bits.

The 64-bit loss already occurs in the classical reduction.  It comes from
guessing which of the adversary's $Q_H$ oracle queries carries the challenge
used in the successful proof, which contributes the factor $Q_H+1$.  No
quantum adversary is needed for this loss.  Thus ``classical loss'' means that
the stated standalone knowledge theorem is already only 55.994 bits against
classical attackers.  It does not mean that the protocol is post-quantum
secure.  The paper gives no quantum-random-oracle reduction and therefore
makes no knowledge-security claim against quantum adversaries.

The standalone implementation profile must be labelled
\emph{100-bit conjectured classical knowledge security}.  Its reduction
ceiling is 55.994 bits before the remaining terms are added.  A protocol with
an unbiased post-commitment beacon could avoid this particular Fiat--Shamir
query loss, and a wider challenge field could increase the margin.  Neither
change is implemented or claimed here.  Grinding, a small-$Q_H$ convention,
generic parallel repetition, and deleting one fold challenge do not repair the
standalone bound.

\section{Extending the ZK construction}\label{sec:extension}

The masking code is reusable, but the ZK claim does not transfer automatically
to another hash, circuit, batch size, or PCS shape.  A new family such as
SHA-256 or Keccak requires all of the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item define the exact relation, public inputs, padding, and fixed hash
      parameters;
\item bind the circuit digest, witness layout, PCS parameters, and complete
      transcript schema;
\item regenerate the symbolic PIOP map and prove a nonzero determinant with an
      explicit all-challenge degree bound;
\item prove that the PCS mask translation is solvable for every query set the
      Flock-ZK verifier can derive, and recompute the unopened-leaf entropy;
\item construct a simulator whose interface accepts only the public statement and
      whose output is accepted by the unchanged family verifier;
\item enumerate every verifier-visible field and prove the hybrid composition,
      including random-oracle programming and grinding order;
\item implement the relation-specific extractor and recompute the complete ZK
      and knowledge-error ledgers; and
\item add a fail-closed certificate entry only after the family-specific
      negative controls and end-to-end certification pass.
\end{enumerate}

The existing SHA-256 and Keccak encoders do not satisfy these obligations merely
by existing.  The algebraic masking strategy is expected to be reusable, but
those statement families remain outside the ZK claim until their own
certificates, simulators, extractors, and error ledgers are established.

\section{Implementation evidence and limitations}\label{sec:evidence}

The paper is accompanied by a
\href{https://github.com/partylikeits1983/flock/tree/ajl/zk-flock}{Flock-ZK
implementation and evidence suite}.  The repository documentation records
exact wire identifiers, circuit digests, schema numbers, artifact paths, build
commands, and benchmark commands.  Those details are kept out of the
mathematical argument because they change with implementation maintenance,
while the claims below depend on their audited contents.

The evidence is deliberately layered.  Lean proves reusable mathematical
statements.  Rust constructs and replays the matrices and translations for the
registered parameters.  The paper supplies the protocol-specific composition
and states the random-oracle assumptions.  Table~\ref{tab:formalization}
separates these roles.

Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:zk} is not presented as a single end-to-end Lean proof.
Lean checks its generic masking, bad-set, and degree-budget ingredients.  The
exact Flock-ZK instantiation also relies on replayable Rust certificates, the
paper proofs above, and the classical programmable-random-oracle model.

The executable evidence ties the numerical claims to the implementation, but
does not replace cryptographic review.  The remaining limits are:
\begin{itemize}
\item The ZK theorem is classical-ROM only; there is no QROM analysis.
\item The standalone knowledge reduction over $\F_{2^{128}}$ is capped at
55.994 bits at $Q_H=2^{64}$ before other terms are added.  This is not a
completed 100-bit theorem.
\item The executable decoder validates candidates but does not implement
general arbitrary-error Reed--Solomon decoding.
\item The exact PCS translator is exercised on a fixture; a standalone proof
for every query set derived by the Flock-ZK verifier remains a review
obligation.
\item Only the two exact registry entries are enabled.  Other Flock statement
families remain non-ZK.
\item Side-channel resistance, recursive composition, and same-prefix
simulation extractability are out of scope.
\end{itemize}

Within these limits, the conclusion is precise: for the registered BLAKE3
batch and fixed-digest relations, Flock now has a public-statement-only
simulator whose proofs are accepted by the Flock-ZK verifier, together with
an explicit distribution argument for every witness-dependent part of the
transcript.  That is the sense in which these Flock profiles are zero
knowledge.
