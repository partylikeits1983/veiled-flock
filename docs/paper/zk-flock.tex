\title{Zero Knowledge for Flock's BLAKE3 Prover:\\
\large Construction, simulation, and concrete bounds}
\author{Alexander Lee}
\date{v7.1, July 2026 --- implementation and security note}

\begin{abstract}
Flock's original BLAKE3 prover \cite{Flock} was not zero knowledge: its transcript exposed
deterministic functions of the witness.  This paper gives a zero-knowledge
variant for exactly two registered production profiles: a batch of 256 BLAKE3
instances and a batch of 256 fixed-digest statements
\[
  y_i=\operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i),
  \qquad |x_i|=64\text{ bytes}.
\]
The verifier is unchanged.

The proof has two algebraic steps.  First, a field-valued mask makes the PIOP
messages independent of the witness after conditioning on the two mask values
that are later opened.  For the production shape, failure occurs on the zero
set of a nonzero polynomial of degree at most 720.  Second, an affine
translation of the PCS blinders preserves every opened value and every
recursive codeword.  These two translations reduce simulation to choosing the
Fiat--Shamir challenges first, solving the dependent messages, and programming
at most 18 fresh random-oracle points.  The simulator receives only the public
statement, and its output is accepted by the production verifier.

In the classical programmable random-oracle model, the explicit algebraic and
programming terms sum to less than $2^{-118.502}$ at a hash-query budget
$Q_H=2^{64}$, before the ordinary 256-bit oracle-collision term.  Thus the two
registered profiles are computationally zero knowledge under the assumptions
stated here.  Knowledge security is separate: the available standalone
Fiat--Shamir reduction over $\F_{2^{128}}$ gives 55.994 bits at the same query
budget.  The stronger 100-bit deployment target is conjectural, not a theorem
of this paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and scope}\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Result in one sentence.}
For the two parameter sets in Table~\ref{tab:profiles}, a proof generated from
a valid witness is computationally indistinguishable from a proof generated
from the public statement alone, in the classical programmable-random-oracle
model.

The fixed-digest relation is
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\rm fd}
  =\left\{
    \bigl((y_1,\ldots,y_{256}),(x_1,\ldots,x_{256})\bigr):
    y_i=\operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i),\ |x_i|=64\text{ bytes}
  \right\}.
\]
The batch relation uses the same BLAKE3 circuit but has no externally fixed
digest list.  In both cases the R1CS shape, packing convention, PCS parameters,
and circuit digest are part of the registered statement family.

Throughout the paper, \emph{computational zero knowledge} has its standard
classical-ROM meaning: for every classical probabilistic polynomial-time
distinguisher making at most $Q_H$ oracle queries, the joint view of an honest
proof and its oracle answers is computationally indistinguishable from the
view produced by a simulator that knows only the statement.  The simulator may
program points that the distinguisher has not already queried.  This is the IOP
and Fiat--Shamir setting of Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, and Spooner
\cite{BCS}; the algebraic masking step is in the spirit of zero-knowledge
sumcheck \cite{CFS17}.

The scope is deliberately narrow.  The preimage circuit hashes one message of
exactly 64 bytes and pins the BLAKE3 IV, counter, block length, and
\texttt{CHUNK\_START}, \texttt{CHUNK\_END}, and \texttt{ROOT} flags.  Unknown
circuit digests, batch sizes, and PCS parameters are rejected before proving.
SHA-256 statements, Keccak statements, longer BLAKE3 messages, hash chains,
Merkle-path statements, recursion, side channels, and quantum random-oracle
queries are not covered.

The argument has a simple structure:
\begin{enumerate}
\item translate the PIOP masks while holding their revealed values fixed;
\item translate the PCS blinders while holding every opening fixed;
\item sample the remaining transcript from the public statement and program
      only fresh, domain-separated oracle points.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~\ref{sec:s2}--\ref{sec:simulation} prove these steps in that order.
Extraction is discussed separately because witness hiding and knowledge
soundness are different properties.

\begin{assumption}[Classical programmable random oracle]\label{ass:rom}
All injectively framed SHA-256 calls are modelled as one random function
$\RO:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{256}$.  The adversary makes classical queries, and
the simulator may program a point only before that point is queried.  No QROM
claim is made.
\end{assumption}

\section{Relation and construction}\label{sec:protocol}

Let $z$ be the packed binary assignment for the block-diagonal BLAKE3 R1CS,
embedded in $\K$.  The prover establishes
\[
  (Az)\circ(Bz)=Cz
\]
using Flock's zerocheck and lincheck, and opens the committed vector with the
ring-switched Ligerito PCS \cite{Ligerito,DP24}.  For the fixed-digest family,
the verifier derives an additional packed opening claim from the public digest
list.  Before the first challenge, the transcript binds that list, the padding
rule, the witness layout, the circuit matrices, the parameter set, and a fresh
32-byte proof nonce.

The ZK prover adds four independent mask sources: the zerocheck mask $P$, the
lincheck mask $S$, and the round-one masks $S_c,S_h$.  The witness and mask
commitments use independent hiding randomness, denoted $\mu$ and $g$.  Every
stream, including the proof nonce, is derived from a per-proof DRBG under a
distinct label.

The main change is the zerocheck mask
\[
  \gamma P(X)Q^\star(X),
  \qquad
  Q^\star(X)=q_0+\sum_j q_jX_j.
\]
Here $P$ is uniform over $\K$ on a translated subcube of the top
$d_{\log}=12$ variables.  The affine polynomial $Q^\star$ is public,
protocol-fixed, and nonzero on the Boolean cube.  The field-valued mask is
essential: the withdrawn Boolean $P,Q$ construction did not support the
Schwartz--Zippel argument needed for production parameters.  Protocol
\texttt{flock-zk-fv-v3} therefore has no $Q$ commitment, $Q$ opening, final
$Q$ evaluation, or $Q$-randomness stream.

\section{One random-oracle boundary}\label{sec:ro}

Fiat--Shamir, Merkle commitments, and proof of work use one oracle, but never
the same input.  Each input begins with a role byte.  A Merkle input also binds
the protocol version, proof nonce, commitment channel, recursion depth, node
level, node index, payload length, and payload.  A proof-of-work input is
\[
  \texttt{ROLE\_POW}\parallel\textit{state}\parallel\textit{nonce}.
\]
The proof nonce is absorbed immediately after the statement and before the
first challenge.

This framing is part of the proof, not merely an encoding convention.  It lets
the extractor identify witness-leaf queries without relying on their order,
prevents simulator programming from aliasing transcript or Merkle inputs, and
makes proofs from different nonces or commitment channels use different
oracle points.  Native, recording, and programming backends implement the same
frames.  When no point is programmed they return identical bytes.  Scalar and
four-way SIMD implementations are differentially tested against the one-shot
definition for every supported tail shape.

\section{The PIOP translation}\label{sec:s2}

Fix the post-commitment challenge tuple $c$.  Let $D$ be the coefficient space
of $P$.  If $n=m-6$ is the number of masked zerocheck rounds, define
\[
  R_c:D\longrightarrow\K^{2n}
\]
to be the map from mask coefficients to the $2n$ round-message coordinates.
Let
\[
  L_c:D\longrightarrow\K^2
\]
map the same coefficients to the two mask values revealed later: the initial
masked claim and $P(\rho)$.  Once the leakage $L_c(P)$ is fixed, the remaining
mask freedom is exactly $\ker L_c$, and the available change in the round
messages is $R_c(\ker L_c)$.

\begin{theorem}[Conditioned field-mask coverage]\label{thm:coverage}
For $m\in\{13,15,22\}$, the symbolic certificate establishes, outside the
zero set of its selected minor,
\[
  \rank\!\begin{bmatrix}R_c\\L_c\end{bmatrix}=2n,
  \qquad
  \rank L_c=2,
  \qquad
  \dim R_c(\ker L_c)=2n-2.
\]
For the production shape $m=22$, the selected determinant is a nonzero
polynomial of total degree at most 720 in the 32 challenge variables.  Hence
\[
  \Pr_c[\text{coverage failure}]
  \le \frac{720}{2^{128}}
  <2^{-118.508}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let $N=\dim D$.  The stacked-rank statement gives
\[
  \dim(\ker R_c\cap\ker L_c)=N-2n,
\]
while $\rank L_c=2$ gives $\dim\ker L_c=N-2$.  Applying rank--nullity to
$R_c$ restricted to $\ker L_c$ yields
\[
  \dim R_c(\ker L_c)
  =(N-2)-(N-2n)=2n-2.
\]
The implementation selects a $2n\times2n$ minor and evaluates its determinant
to a nonzero field element at one explicit challenge tuple.  Its determinant
is therefore not the zero polynomial.  The generic symbolic kernels bound its
total degree by 720, so Schwartz--Zippel gives the stated bad-set probability
\cite{Schwartz,Zippel}.
\end{proof}

The concrete and symbolic matrices come from the same generic round kernels.
The symbolic scalar has three interpretations: evaluation in $\K$,
conservative multivariate degree bounds, and exact sparse polynomials for small
instances.  It has no inversion operation, so a challenge-dependent
denominator cannot enter the calculation unnoticed.  The selected rows,
columns, nonzero evaluation, and degree vector are pinned in
\texttt{s2\_mask\_coverage.json} and replayed by the certificate suite.

Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} proves the PIOP masking step.  It says nothing by
itself about privacy at the PCS opening boundary; that is the next step.

\section{The PCS translation}\label{sec:s3}

Write the initial combined opening vector as
\[
  F=(\mu\parallel z)+c\,g,
\]
where $c\in\K$ is sampled after commitment.  Let $d$ be a witness difference
that preserves every public opening claim.  The aim is to replace $z$ by
$z+d$ without changing anything the verifier opens.

\begin{lemma}[Opening-preserving translation]\label{lem:pcs}
Suppose $c\ne0$ and the initial-level query system has full row rank.  There is
an affine bijection on the uniform PCS masks such that replacing $z$ by $z+d$
leaves the queried initial rows and the entire combined vector $F$ unchanged.
Consequently every recursive codeword, root, authentication path, sumcheck
value, and out-of-domain sample is unchanged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
  \delta g_{\rm top}=c^{-1}d.
\]
Choose $\delta\mu$ to solve the queried initial-level encoding equations, and
set
\[
  \delta g_{\rm lo}=c^{-1}\delta\mu.
\]
Because the field has characteristic two,
$d+c\,\delta g_{\rm top}=0$ and
$\delta\mu+c\,\delta g_{\rm lo}=0$.  Thus $\delta F=0$ globally.  The defining
equations for $\delta\mu$ also hold the separately opened initial rows fixed.
Translation by these deltas is a bijection on uniform masks, so it preserves
their distribution.
\end{proof}

The production opening queries 218 initial-level positions; each lane contains
512 independent mask symbols.  After conditioning on all opened positions,
each additional fresh wide leaf retains at least 16,384 bits of conditional
entropy.  No recursive entropy charge is needed because Lemma~\ref{lem:pcs}
makes the recursive codewords identical.  The exceptional event $c=0$ has
probability $2^{-128}$.

The proof format contains twelve categories of opening fields.  The
implementation exhaustively destructures and classifies them, so adding an
unclassified field fails compilation.  The exact translator is tested against
the production additive NTT and wide interleaving.  Its functional manifest
and entropy table are pinned in
\path{s3_opening_functionals.json} and
\path{s3_minentropy_table.json}.

\section{Simulator and concrete ZK bound}\label{sec:simulation}

Once Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} and Lemma~\ref{lem:pcs} are separated, the
simulator is direct.  It receives a public-statement capability containing the
setup and digest list, but no witness type.  It constructs an unrelated trace,
overwrites the public-output region, samples the terminal zerocheck values, and
solves the dependent messages in one pass.  The patched trace is not an R1CS
witness: running the honest prover on it is rejected.  After the simulator
programs the required fresh oracle points, however, the production verifier
accepts the simulated proof.  The simulator programs at most 18 points.

The hybrid argument is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:games}.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{Game changes and their explicit charges.}
\label{tab:games}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Game & Change & Charge \\
\midrule
$G_0$ & honest proof & $0$ \\
$G_1$ & translate the PIOP mask & $720/2^{128}$ \\
$G_2$ & translate $\mu$ and $g$ & $1/2^{128}$ \\
$G_3$ & cross the Merkle boundary & $Q_H/2^{16384}+\varepsilon_{\rm RO}$ \\
$G_4$ & program fresh challenges & $18Q_H/2^{256}$ \\
$G_5$ & solve the terminal messages & $2/2^{128}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The three algebraic numerators are $720$, $1$, and $2$: the symbolic bad set,
$c=0$, and the two rejected values of the terminal challenge.  Thus the part
of the bound tracked explicitly by the executable ledger is
\[
  \varepsilon_{\rm explicit}(Q_H)
  =
  \frac{723}{2^{128}}
  +\frac{18Q_H}{2^{256}}
  +\frac{Q_H}{2^{16384}}.
\]
At $Q_H=2^{64}$,
\[
  -\log_2\varepsilon_{\rm explicit}(2^{64})
  =118.502\ldots.
\]
The term $\varepsilon_{\rm RO}$ denotes the ordinary collision and binding
term at the framed 256-bit oracle boundary.  It is negligible for the stated
classical query regime, but it is not included in the quoted 118.502-bit
figure.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped computational zero knowledge]\label{thm:zk}
Assume the classical programmable random oracle of Assumption \ref{ass:rom},
the conditioned coverage of Theorem \ref{thm:coverage}, the opening
translation of Lemma \ref{lem:pcs}, and freshness of the programmed points.
Then both profiles in Table \ref{tab:profiles} are computationally zero
knowledge.  For every classical distinguisher making at most $Q_H$ oracle
queries,
\[
  \Adv_{\rm ZK}
  \le \varepsilon_{\rm explicit}(Q_H)+\varepsilon_{\rm RO}.
\]
The simulator uses only the public statement, and the verifier is the
production verifier.
\end{theorem}

This theorem is in the classical programmable-ROM.  It is not a QROM theorem,
and it does not turn the ideal-oracle argument into a standard-model theorem
for SHA-256.

\section{Extraction and simulation extractability}\label{sec:extract}

Zero knowledge says that a proof reveals no witness.  Knowledge soundness asks
the complementary question: can a successful prover be used to recover one?
The two properties use different arguments and have different concrete
bounds.

The recording oracle observes the framed leaf queries used to construct the
witness commitment.  Given a complete valid codeword, the extractor rebuilds
the interleaved word, applies the inverse NTT lane by lane, re-encodes the
candidate, checks the configured unique-radius condition, and returns the
witness half.  The system-specific step then reads the 512 message bits of
each instance and independently verifies
\[
  \operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i)=y_i.
\]
Honest recorded commitments yield the true preimages; simulated commitments
do not extract.

The executable decoder is an exact-word decoder and candidate validator.  It
is not a complete Reed--Solomon decoder for every noisy word inside the
theoretical radius.  The general noisy-word step therefore comes from the
Ligerito proximity and unique-decoding argument, not from the current decoder
routine.

For simulation extractability, the simulator records
\[
  (\textit{statement digest},\textit{proof nonce},\textit{protocol version})
\]
for each simulated proof.  Extraction refuses every recorded prefix and is
attempted only on a fresh one.  The implemented property is therefore
\emph{fresh-prefix weak simulation extractability}.  Same-prefix simulation
extractability is not claimed.

\section{Knowledge-security profiles}\label{sec:knowledge}

The knowledge ledger contains an interactive L0 fold term
\[
  \kappa_\Gamma=\frac{257}{2^{128}},
\]
or 119.994 bits.  The classical Fiat--Shamir reduction contributes the factor
$Q_H+1$.  At $Q_H=2^{64}$ it therefore yields
\[
  -\log_2\bigl((Q_H+1)\kappa_\Gamma\bigr)
  =55.994\ldots\text{ bits}.
\]
This loss is classical; it is not a post-quantum penalty.

The shipped standalone profile must be labelled
\emph{100-bit conjectured classical knowledge security}.  The theorem column
remains 55.994 bits.  An unbiased post-commitment beacon removes the
Fiat--Shamir query loss but changes the protocol model.  A challenge-only
$\F_{2^{256}}$ profile would restore a wide standalone margin, but the required
machinery does not exist in this implementation.  Grinding, a small-$Q_H$
convention, generic parallel repetition, and deleting one fold challenge do
not repair the standalone bound.

\section{Reproduction and limitations}\label{sec:implementation}

The wire protocol is \texttt{flock-zk-fv-v3}; proof I/O version 6 and
transcript schema version 5 bind the nonce, oracle frames, and field-mask
schema.  The two registered circuit digests are:
\begin{verbatim}
BLAKE3 batch:
4a398ca2e73d9d1f70611bd6a2a408b0404aabddcf6cf75c29cfa412f72f8423
BLAKE3 fixed digest:
e71bde2b053b1b697412d2d30c2e2e57f7b5f1822484fcbcc15c89ea202890d5
\end{verbatim}

The complete evidence gate is:
\begin{verbatim}
rustup override set stable
scripts/zk-certify.sh
cargo test --release --workspace --features zk,symbolic
cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --features zk,symbolic -- -D warnings
cd lean && lake build
\end{verbatim}
It replays the symbolic artifacts, transcript schema, oracle framing,
production rank gates, simulator, extractor, fixed-digest and batch
roundtrips, Lean adapters, knowledge ledger, and the ban on unframed direct
SHA-256 calls.  Evidence names are compared with script test names in both
directions.

The executable evidence is useful because it ties each numerical claim to the
shipped code.  It is not a substitute for a cryptographic proof or for
independent review.  The remaining limits are:
\begin{itemize}
\item The ZK theorem is classical-ROM only; there is no QROM analysis.
\item The standalone knowledge reduction over $\F_{2^{128}}$ gives 55.994
bits at $Q_H=2^{64}$, not a 100-bit theorem.
\item The executable decoder validates candidates but does not implement
general arbitrary-error Reed--Solomon decoding.
\item Only the two exact registry entries are enabled.  Other Flock statement
families remain non-ZK.
\item Side-channel resistance, recursive composition, and same-prefix
simulation extractability are out of scope.
\item The construction and proof have not received independent cryptographic
review.
\end{itemize}

Within these limits, the conclusion is precise: for the registered BLAKE3
batch and fixed-digest relations, Flock now has a public-statement-only
simulator whose proofs are accepted by the production verifier, together with
an explicit distribution argument for every witness-dependent part of the
transcript.  That is the sense in which these Flock profiles are zero
knowledge.
