\title{Making Flock Zero-Knowledge:\\
\large Fixed-digest BLAKE3, symbolic coverage, and an executable ROM game}
\author{Alexander Lee}
\date{v7, July 2026 --- implementation and security note}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Flock's original BLAKE3 prover reveals deterministic functions of its witness.
This note specifies and implements a zero-knowledge mode for two exact
production profiles: the free BLAKE3 batch relation and, more importantly, the
fixed-digest relation
\[
  y_i=\operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i),\qquad |x_i|=64\text{ bytes},\quad 1\le i\le256.
\]
The construction uses field-valued masking over $\K$, a public affine
$Q^\star$, a hiding Ligerito commitment, a proof nonce, and one framed random
oracle shared by Fiat--Shamir, Merkle trees, and proof of work.  A symbolic
coverage calculation emits a nonzero minor of total-degree bound 720; hence
the production PIOP bad set has probability at most $720/2^{128}$.  A
closed-form PCS translator preserves the combined opening vector and leaves
16,384 bits of conditional entropy in each fresh initial wide leaf.  A sealed,
one-pass simulator sees only the public digest statement, programs at most 18
fresh oracle points, and produces a proof accepted by the unmodified verifier.

Under the classical programmable-random-oracle model and the assumptions made
explicit below, this gives \emph{computational zero knowledge} for the two
registered circuit digests.  At an adversarial hash-query budget $Q=2^{64}$,
the symbolic PIOP term has 118.508 bits and the explicit listed union bound is
118.502 bits.  Knowledge is a separate claim: the shipped
standalone profile has about 55.994 bits of currently provable classical
knowledge security after the Fiat--Shamir loss and is labelled
\emph{100-bit conjectured classical knowledge security}.  It does not meet a
100-bit standalone theorem threshold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and security labels}\label{sec:scope}

The certificate registry contains exactly the following profiles.  Unknown
families, circuit digests, batch sizes, or PCS parameters are rejected before
proof generation.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Family & Public input & Circuit / PCS & ZK status \\
\midrule
BLAKE3 batch & batch size 256 & $m=22$, rate $1/2$, batch log 6 & computational ZK \\
BLAKE3 preimage & 256 digests & $m=22$, rate $1/2$, batch log 6 & computational ZK \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The preimage family covers one full BLAKE3 hash of exactly 64 bytes.  Its R1CS
pins the IV, counter, block length, and \texttt{CHUNK\_START},
\texttt{CHUNK\_END}, and \texttt{ROOT} flags.  SHA-256, Keccak, longer BLAKE3
messages, chains, Merkle-path statements, recursion, side channels, and the
QROM are out of scope.  No claim in this note transfers to those cases.

\paragraph{Mandatory separation of claims.}
``Computational ZK'' describes witness hiding.  ``Knowledge security''
describes extraction.  The shipped standalone profile may be described as
\emph{100-bit conjectured classical knowledge security}; its theorem column
must remain 55.994 bits at $Q=2^{64}$.  Section~\ref{sec:knowledge} gives the
three permitted knowledge profiles.

\begin{assumption}[Classical programmable random oracle]\label{ass:rom}
SHA-256, used with the injective frames in Section~\ref{sec:ro}, is modelled as
a classical random oracle.  The simulator may program fresh, previously
unqueried points.  Collision resistance and pseudorandomness hide unopened
Merkle leaves and siblings.  Quantum queries are excluded.
\end{assumption}

\section{The relation and protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

Let $z$ be the packed binary assignment for the block-diagonal BLAKE3 R1CS,
embedded into $\K$.  The prover establishes
\[
  (Az)\circ(Bz)=Cz
\]
with Flock's zerocheck and lincheck, then opens the committed vector using the
ring-switched Ligerito PCS.  In the fixed-digest family the verifier also
derives a packed-direct opening claim from the public digest list.  The list,
padding policy, witness layout, and circuit matrices are transcript-bound
before the first challenge.

The zero-knowledge prover adds independent randomizer rows and four committed
mask sources: the field-valued zerocheck mask $P$, the lincheck mask $S$, and
the round-one masks $S_c,S_h$.  The witness commitment and each mask
commitment use independent $\mu$ and $g$ hiding randomness.  All streams,
including the proof nonce, are forks of one per-proof DRBG under distinct
labels.

The zerocheck mask is
\[
  \gamma P(X)Q^\star(X),
  \qquad
  Q^\star(X)=q_0+\sum_j q_jX_j,
\]
where $P$ is uniform over $\K$ on a translated subcube of the top
$d_{\log}=12$ variables and $Q^\star$ is protocol-fixed, public, affine, and
nonzero on every Boolean cube point.  This replaces the withdrawn construction
with two Boolean masks $P,Q$.  There is no $Q$ commitment, opening, final
evaluation, or mask-randomness fork in protocol \texttt{flock-zk-fv-v3}.

\section{One framed random oracle}\label{sec:ro}

Every hash point begins with a disjoint role byte.  Merkle points additionally
bind protocol version, proof nonce, commitment channel, recursion depth, node
level, node index, payload length, and payload.  Proof-of-work points are
\[
  \texttt{ROLE\_POW}\parallel\textit{state}\parallel\textit{nonce}.
\]
Fiat--Shamir absorbs the 32-byte proof nonce immediately after the statement
and before any challenge.  Native hashing and external recording/programming
backends consume identical frames and return byte-identical answers when no
point is programmed.

These frames serve three purposes.  First, the Merkle extractor can recognize
every witness leaf query without relying on observation order.  Second,
simulator programming cannot alias a leaf, internal node, transcript squeeze,
or proof-of-work query.  Third, reusing a proof across nonces, commitment
channels, or recursive depths changes every relevant oracle point.  Scalar and
four-way SIMD midstate implementations are tested against the one-shot
definition for every tail shape.

\section{Symbolic PIOP coverage}\label{sec:s2}

Fix the challenges after the commitment.  Let $R$ map the coefficients of $P$
to the $2n$ zerocheck round-message coordinates, and let $L$ map them to the
two mask values revealed later: the initial masked claim and $P(\rho)$.  For a
fixed leakage value the available displacement is $R(\ker L)$.

\begin{theorem}[Conditioned field-mask coverage]\label{thm:coverage}
For $m\in\{13,15,22\}$, the emitted symbolic matrix satisfies, outside its
selected minor's zero set,
\[
  \begin{gathered}
    \rank\begin{bmatrix}R\\L\end{bmatrix}=2n,
    \qquad \rank L=2,\\
    \dim R(\ker L)=2n-2.
  \end{gathered}
\]
At the production shape $m=22$, the selected determinant has total degree at
most 720 in the $32$ challenge variables.  Uniform challenges therefore give
\[
  \Pr[\text{coverage failure}]\le \frac{720}{2^{128}}
  <2^{-118.508}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The implementation derives both the concrete and symbolic matrices from the
same generic round kernels.  \texttt{SymScalar} has three backends: concrete
$\K$ evaluation, conservative per-variable tropical degree bounds, and exact
sparse multivariate polynomials for toy instances.  It deliberately exposes no
inversion operation.  Thus challenge-dependent denominators cannot silently
enter the polynomial calculation.

The S2 JSON artifact pins the selected rows, columns, nonzero determinant
evaluations, and degree vectors.  One Lean adapter instantiates Mathlib's
sum-of-per-variable-degree Schwartz--Zippel theorem; another proves the
bad-set mixture step.  This theorem covers the field-mask block; it does not
by itself claim PCS opening privacy.

\section{PCS replacement and entropy}\label{sec:s3}

Write the initial hiding commitment's combined vector as
\[
  F=(\mu\parallel z)+c\,g,
\]
where $c\in\K$ is transcript-derived.  For any claim-preserving witness
difference $d$ and $c\ne0$, the translator sets
\[
  \delta g_{\mathrm{top}}=c^{-1}d
\]
so that $\delta F_{\mathrm{top}}=0$.  It solves the selected initial-level
encoding equations for $\delta\mu$ and takes
$\delta g_{\mathrm{lo}}=c^{-1}\delta\mu$, making every opened initial wide row
identical as well.  Characteristic two removes sign distinctions in these
equalities.

The production opening makes 218 initial-level queries while each lane has
512 independent mask symbols.  After conditioning on an opened wide leaf, at
least 16,384 bits remain.  Since $\delta F=0$ globally, every recursive
codeword, root, sibling, sumcheck value, and out-of-domain sample is identical;
no recursive entropy charge is needed.  The only algebraic exceptional event
is $c=0$, with probability $2^{-128}$.

The implementation exhaustively destructures all opening proof fields into a
12-category manifest.  Adding a proof field without classifying it is a compile
failure.  The exact translator is differentially tested against the shipped
NTT and wide interleaving.  The pinned artifacts are
\texttt{s3\_opening\_functionals.json} and
\texttt{s3\_minentropy\_table.json}.

\section{The sealed simulator game}\label{sec:simulation}

The simulator accepts a capability \texttt{SealedStatement} whose private
fields contain only the setup and public digests; its function type cannot
name a witness or preimage.  In one pass it constructs a trace for unrelated
messages, overwrites the public-output region, samples the zerocheck terminal
values, and solves the dependent messages.  The patched trace is not a valid
R1CS witness, and the honest prover run on it is rejected.  Nevertheless, after
programming the required fresh oracle points, the unmodified production
verifier accepts the simulated proof.

The executable game ledger consists of:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Game & Change & Charged term \\
\midrule
$G_0$ & honest proof & 0 \\
$G_1$ & translate PIOP masks & $720/2^{128}$ \\
$G_2$ & translate $\mu,g$ & $\Pr[c=0]+\varepsilon_{\rm solve}$ \\
$G_3$ & replace hidden Merkle leaves & $\varepsilon_{\rm sibling}+\varepsilon_{\rm hash}$ \\
$G_4$ & pre-sample and program challenges & $\varepsilon_{\rm prequery}+\varepsilon_{\rm prefix}$ \\
$G_5$ & sealed one-pass simulator & degenerate-challenge terms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At $Q=2^{64}$ adversarial oracle queries and at most 18 programmed points, the
prequery term has 187.830 bits, the initial sibling term has more than 16,300
bits, and the symbolic rank term has 118.508 bits.  Summing the listed terms
gives 118.502 bits before conventional hash-collision terms.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped computational zero knowledge]\label{thm:zk}
Assume the classical programmable-RO model, Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage}, the PCS
translator and entropy bounds of Section~\ref{sec:s3}, and freshness of the
programmed points.  Then the registered fixed-digest profile is computationally
zero knowledge, with distinguishing advantage bounded by the sum of the six
game terms above.  The registered free-batch profile follows as the easier
self-generated-witness special case.
\end{theorem}

This is a classical-ROM theorem.  It is neither a QROM result nor a claim that
SHA-256 is an information-theoretic random oracle.

\section{Extraction and simulation extractability}\label{sec:extract}

The point-oracle recorder observes all framed leaf queries made while building
the witness commitment.  For a complete valid codeword it reconstructs the
interleaved word, applies the inverse NTT lane by lane, re-encodes the candidate,
checks the configured unique-radius inequality, and returns the witness half;
the low mask and blinder lanes are separate outputs.  The system-specific
extractor then reads exactly the 512 message bits of each instance and checks
\[
  \operatorname{BLAKE3}(x_i)=y_i
\]
with the external \texttt{blake3} implementation.  This final check refers to
no circuit wire or proof claim.  Honest recorded commitments recover the real
preimages; the simulator's commitment fails extraction.

The executable decoder is a straight-line candidate validator and exact-word
decoder, not a complete Reed--Solomon error-correction implementation for every
received word within the theoretical radius.  The general noisy-word step is
therefore supplied by the Ligerito proximity/unique-decoding theorem, not by a
claim that the current routine corrects arbitrary errors.

For simulation extractability, the simulator records the prefix
\[
  (\textit{statement digest},\textit{proof nonce},\textit{protocol version}).
\]
Extraction is allowed only on a fresh prefix and refuses a prefix returned by
the simulation oracle.  This is \emph{fresh-prefix weak simulation
extractability}.  Same-prefix simulation extractability and straight-line
random-beacon-resistant knowledge are not claimed.

\section{Knowledge security profiles}\label{sec:knowledge}

The interactive opening has an L0 fold access-structure term at least
$257/2^{128}$, corresponding to 119.994 bits.  The classical Fiat--Shamir
knowledge reduction charges $(Q+1)\kappa_\Gamma$.  At $Q=2^{64}$, this term
alone limits the standalone theorem to
\[
  128-\log_2 257-64 = 55.994\text{ bits}.
\]
This loss is classical, not post-quantum.

The repository pins both the interactive and post-Fiat--Shamir columns in
\path{docs/artifacts/knowledge-ledger.json}.  Running
\path{scripts/knowledge-ledger.py} with \texttt{--check} regenerates and
compares them.
Grinding, a small-$Q$ convention, generic parallel repetition, or omitting an
individual fold challenge does not repair the standalone bound.

\section{Implementation, reproduction, and limits}\label{sec:implementation}

The wire protocol is \texttt{flock-zk-fv-v3}; proof I/O version 6 and transcript
schema version 5 bind the proof nonce, framed oracle domains, and P-only mask
schema.  The exact registered circuit digests are:
\begin{verbatim}
BLAKE3 batch:
4a398ca2e73d9d1f70611bd6a2a408b0404aabddcf6cf75c29cfa412f72f8423
BLAKE3 fixed digest:
e71bde2b053b1b697412d2d30c2e2e57f7b5f1822484fcbcc15c89ea202890d5
\end{verbatim}

The certification command is fail-fatal and rejects zero-test filters:
\begin{verbatim}
rustup override set stable
scripts/zk-certify.sh
cargo test --release --workspace --features zk,symbolic
cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --features zk,symbolic -- -D warnings
cd lean && lake build
\end{verbatim}
It replays the symbolic artifacts, transcript schema, RO framing, production
rank gates, simulator, extractor, fixed-digest and batch roundtrips, Lean
adapters, knowledge ledger, and the ban on direct SHA-256 calls outside the two
reviewed oracle modules.  Certificate evidence names are compared with script
test names in both directions.

The following limitations remain material:
\begin{itemize}
\item The proof relies on the classical programmable-RO abstraction and has no
QROM analysis.
\item The knowledge reduction does not prove 100 standalone bits over
$\F_{2^{128}}$; the precise label in Section~\ref{sec:knowledge} is mandatory.
\item The executable decoder validates a candidate but does not implement a
complete arbitrary-error RS decoder.
\item Only 64-byte BLAKE3 preimages and the two exact registry entries are
enabled.  Other Flock statement families remain non-zk.
\item Side-channel resistance, recursive composition, and same-prefix
simulation extractability are out of scope.
\item The construction and proof have not received independent cryptographic
review.  The executable evidence is a reproducibility aid, not a substitute
for review.
\end{itemize}

Subject to these limits, the implementation now has the object absent from the
earlier drafts: a public-statement-only simulator whose output is accepted by
the unmodified verifier, together with a symbolic distribution argument and a
fail-closed registry for the exact deployed shapes.  That is the scope in which
Flock is zero knowledge.
