\title{Zero-Knowledge Proofs of BLAKE3 Preimage Knowledge in Flock\\
\large A fixed-digest relation, a simulator, and an extractor}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Earlier work made Flock's BLAKE3 \emph{batch} statement zero-knowledge. That
statement binds no public input: it asserts only that some valid compressions
exist, and its simulator is the honest prover on a witness it chooses itself.
This note reports the statement applications actually want --- for public
digests $y_1,\dots,y_n$, knowledge of preimages $x_1,\dots,x_n$ with
$\blake(x_i)=y_i$ --- together with both halves of the security property: a
simulator that receives only the digests and whose output the unmodified
verifier accepts, and an extractor that recovers real preimages and
\emph{fails} on the simulator's own output. We describe the construction, the
measurements behind it, and --- at least as importantly --- the methodology,
including the design errors the methodology caught.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why the previous statement was not enough}

The batch statement's public input is the batch size. Its digest covers the
matrices and layout; the verifier receives the setup, the commitment and the
proof, and compares the accepted claim values against nothing. So the assertion
is ``there exist $2^n$ valid BLAKE3 compressions'', which anyone can satisfy.

Every zero-knowledge result for that statement inherits the weakness, because
its simulator is the honest prover run on a self-generated witness --- legitimate
\emph{precisely} because nothing constrains the witness. Two consequences bite
as soon as a digest is pinned:

\begin{itemize}
\item a simulator that could produce a witness for a \emph{given} $y$ would be
inverting $\blake$;
\item the preimage is essentially unique, so
\textbf{witness-indistinguishability is vacuous} --- and witness-indistinguishability
is what every coverage certificate in the previous work measures.
\end{itemize}

The fixed-digest statement is therefore a new object, not a parameter change,
and none of the earlier evidence transfers unexamined.

\section{The relation}

\subsection{Making one compression a whole hash}

$\blake$ of a message fitting a single 64-byte block is one compression with
$cv = IV$, $\text{counter} = 0$, $\text{block\_len} = 64$ and
$\text{flags} = \textsc{chunk\_start}\,|\,\textsc{chunk\_end}\,|\,\textsc{root}$.
We pin exactly those wires. Since the R1CS is in circuit shape ($C=I$), row $i$
asserts $(Az)_i (Bz)_i = z_i$, so a slot is forced to one by $1\cdot 1 = z_s$ and
to zero by $0 \cdot 1 = z_s$; no new mechanism is needed. The message words stay
free: they are the witness. The pinning lives in $A_0, B_0$ and hence in the
statement digest, so a proof for the free circuit can never be read as a proof
for this one.

\paragraph{An implementation trap worth recording.} Pinning a bit to zero
\emph{must} keep the constant wire on the $b$-side. The alternative
$0\cdot 0 = z_s$ pins equally well and satisfies the R1CS --- and breaks every
honest proof. The witness generator emits $b[s]=1$ for parameter slots, so the
Hadamard product still matched while the $B z$ \emph{vector} did not, and the
lincheck, which checks the matrix--vector products rather than only their
product, rejected. The symptom was a lincheck failure on a witness that
satisfies the R1CS.

\subsection{Binding the digests}

The output region $\textit{out\_lo}$ occupies an aligned 256-bit slot of every
block. The digest list is therefore a \emph{public} assignment to that slot
across instances, and binding it is one multilinear identity checked inside the
\emph{existing} batched opening:
\[
  \hat z_{\text{packed}}(\tau,\ \textsc{sel}=\textsc{out},\ \iota)
  \;=\; \hat y(\tau,\iota),
\]
where $\hat y$ is the multilinear extension of the public digests. The
\textbf{verifier computes the target itself}, so it is not a proof field and
there is nothing for a prover to move; soundness is Schwartz--Zippel at
$(n_{\log}+2)/2^{128}$.

\paragraph{Why a random point and not the digest's own indices.} The obvious
binding --- open the witness at the digest bit positions --- leaks. A
ring-switched claim at a Boolean point publishes $\hat s_v$, whose entries are
then literally the 128 raw bits of the witness word; a packed-direct claim at a
Boolean point reveals that whole word. Evaluating at a random point reveals only
$\hat y(\tau)$, which is public. This is what makes the binding compatible with
hiding: conditioning the privacy argument on a public value concedes nothing.

\subsection{Padding, and a measurement that contradicted an assumption}

The batch is padded to $2^{n_{\log}}$ instances. Padding slots are not free: the
constant-wire pin already required every block to carry a valid compression, and
under a pinning they must satisfy the pinned rows too. So a padding slot holds
the hash of the all-zero message, and its digest is $\blake(0^{64})$ ---
\textbf{not zero}. A statement that assumed zero padding would compute the wrong
target and reject honest proofs. The padding rule is consequently part of the
statement hash.

\subsection{Transcript binding}

The digest list, its order, the padding rule and the geometry are absorbed
before any challenge, so every challenge --- and hence the proof --- is specific
to this statement. This also closes a gap the older chain statement still has:
its public endpoints are checked arithmetically but never absorbed, so its
challenges do not depend on them.

\section{The simulator}

\begin{definition}[NIZK]
There is a PPT simulator $\mathsf{S}$ such that for every statement $y$ and
every witness $x$ with $\blake(x_i)=y_i$, the distributions of $\mathsf{P}^H(y,x)$
and $\mathsf{S}^{H[\cdot]}(y)$ are computationally indistinguishable to a
distinguisher with oracle access to $H$, where $\mathsf{S}$ may program $H$.
\end{definition}

$\mathsf{S}$ receives only $y$: no preimage, no trace, no trapdoor --- the
system is transparent and there is none to give.

\subsection{Construction}

The fixed-digest relation admits a construction simpler than the general
recipe. $\mathsf{S}$ commits an honest trace for messages \emph{of its own
choosing}, with the output region overwritten by the public digests. That vector
satisfies the digest claim by construction and is \emph{not} a satisfying R1CS
assignment. Consequently:

\begin{itemize}
\item the lincheck, the six hiding commitments and the batched opening all run
\textbf{unmodified production code} on it --- they speak about whatever was
committed;
\item only the zerocheck, the one sub-proof that would notice, is emitted.
\end{itemize}

The emitted zerocheck uses exactly the freedom the verifier leaves. The
$C$-side evaluation is \emph{recomputed} by the verifier as the interpolation of
$\textit{round1}_c$ at $z$, so that vector must interpolate to the committed
vector's true $C$-value --- one linear condition on a 64-vector, solved in one
coordinate. $\textit{round1}_{ab}$ is free. Each multilinear round is free
except the last $G(\infty)$, which is solved so the telescoped claim lands
exactly on $\hat a(\rho)\hat b(\rho) + \gamma P(\rho)Q(\rho)$.

\paragraph{Why programming is necessary and not decorative.} That final solve
needs $\rho$ \emph{before} the message preceding it is emitted, and plain
Fiat--Shamir samples $\rho$ only after. The ordering obstruction is real. With a
programmable oracle the challenge is fixed in advance and the solve becomes
available. The implementation programs 18 points ($z$, $\gamma$, and one per
multilinear round); $r_{\text{skip}}$ and $r_{\text{outer}}$ are deliberately
left honest, since the terminal evaluations depend only on the fold point.

\paragraph{Two passes.} The emitter needs the committed vector's true terminal
evaluations before it can telescope to them. Pass one runs the prover honestly
against a throwaway oracle and records them, adopting its (uniform) challenges
as the chosen tuple; pass two emits.

\subsection{Three constructions that do not work}

Recording the failures is part of the method, because two of them look correct.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Run the honest prover on a self-chosen witness.} The batch
simulator. The digest claim pins $\hat z$ on the output region to the public
digests; a self-chosen message hashes elsewhere.
\item \emph{Patch a valid witness and prove honestly.} The patched vector does
not satisfy the R1CS, so the honest zerocheck does not vanish and the verifier
rejects. (This is now a regression test.)
\item \emph{Sample every revealed value from the coset the verifier's equations
cut out.} This is the one that looks right. Per level the Ligerito verifier
checks a single batched terminal identity, so that coset is strictly larger than
the set of honest transcripts: honest opened rows are rows of a Reed--Solomon
codeword, and at deep levels the query count exceeds the per-lane message
dimension, so they satisfy public code-parity relations the verifier never
tests. A distinguisher recomputes one parity functional and separates, with no
oracle queries at all.
\end{enumerate}

Construction 3 is why the implemented simulator \emph{computes} the opening
rather than sampling it.

\section{Distribution equality}

Acceptance is necessary, not sufficient: a simulator whose output verifies but
is distributed differently is still broken. The simulated transcript is uniform
on the variety the verifier's checks cut out, so the two laws coincide exactly
when the \emph{honest} transcript is uniform on that same variety. Class by
class:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Honest} & \textbf{Evidence} \\
\midrule
zerocheck round pairs & $G_j + \gamma M_j$ & channel spans $4096/4096$ bits at $m{=}22$ \\
degenerate control & --- & constant mask spans exactly $2048$ \\
terminals $\hat a,\hat b$ & moved by randomizer rows & measured across fresh draws \\
$\sigma_z$ & $\sum \mathrm{eq}\cdot PQ$, fresh $P,Q$ & measured across fresh draws \\
digest claim & public function of $y$ & public by construction \\
opening interior & blinded by $\mu$, $g$ & existing low-mask / blinder lemmas \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Full rank on the round-pair block is the load-bearing measurement: it says the
honest round messages are uniform over that block \emph{whatever the witness
was}, which is exactly the law the simulator samples. The degenerate control ---
a constant mask reaching the $G(1)$ half and no more --- shows the measurement is
not vacuous.

\paragraph{Claim-kernel form.} These certificates quantify over \emph{masks},
not over witness pairs. That is a necessity rather than a preference: for a
unique-witness relation the witness-pair form is vacuous, so the earlier
certificates could not have been reused even in principle.

\section{Knowledge extraction}

Zero knowledge says a proof reveals nothing; it says nothing about whether the
prover knew anything. Building a simulator before an extractor is the dangerous
order, since a protocol that hides everything and proves nothing passes every
zero-knowledge test.

\begin{definition}[Straightline ROM extraction]
There is a PPT $\mathsf{E}$ which, given a prover's random-oracle query
transcript and an accepting proof for $y$, outputs $x_1,\dots,x_n$ with
$\blake(x_i)=y_i$ except with negligible probability.
\end{definition}

The extractor has two halves. \emph{Recovering the committed message} is the
standard BCS argument: every committed Merkle leaf is an oracle query, so the
query transcript yields the codeword, which decodes. We cite this rather than
re-derive it. \emph{Turning the committed message into preimages} is
system-specific and is where a mistake would hide: which coordinates are the
message, how they are packed, and whether what emerges is real. That half is
implemented, and it ends with an external $\blake(x_i) = y_i$ check that
touches no circuit wire, no claim value and no part of the proof.

The external recheck rules out, simultaneously, the three near-misses that would
each look like success: recovering \emph{mask columns} (the randomizer rows and
five mask cubes are committed data too), recovering an assignment untied to the
digests, and recovering an opening claim instead of the message.

\paragraph{The test that separates the two properties.} \textbf{Extraction fails
on the simulator's commitment.} The simulator produces a transcript the verifier
accepts and knows no preimage; if extraction succeeded there, the extractor
would be recovering something other than knowledge and the claim would be empty.
Alongside it: extraction recovers the real preimages from an honest commitment,
and perturbing the randomizer sections moves the commitment without moving one
extracted byte.

\section{Methodology}

The technical content above is less transferable than the method that produced
it, so we state the method explicitly.

\paragraph{Take the verifier's checks as the specification.} The simulator was
not designed from the protocol's intent but from the verifier's code: each check
was read as a constraint, each constraint classified as \emph{determined} or
\emph{free}, and the free ones filled with the dependent ones solved. This is
what makes the emitted zerocheck correct by construction rather than by
argument, and it is what exposed the ordering obstruction --- a fact about the
check order that no amount of reasoning about the protocol's purpose would have
surfaced.

\paragraph{Every claim gets a control that must fail.} An assertion that
something passes is worth little without a companion showing the test can fail.
The controls here are load-bearing and several caught real errors:

\begin{itemize}
\item an \emph{unprogrammed} oracle must reproduce plain Fiat--Shamir. This
caught the harness absorbing the proof-of-work nonce as raw bytes where the real
challenger tags it --- every challenge after the first grind was silently
desynchronised, and six unit tests had passed because none of them grind.
\item the patched vector must \emph{fail} the R1CS, and an \emph{honest} prover
on it must be \emph{rejected}, or the simulator's acceptance would prove
nothing.
\item a degenerate mask must span strictly less than the full block, or the
coverage measurement would be vacuous.
\item extraction must fail on the simulator.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Keep the demonstration on the real code path.} A simulator that runs
a reimplementation demonstrates nothing about the prover, and a parallel
orchestration drifts. The prover therefore gained exactly one seam --- a
zerocheck source that defaults to the honest implementation and receives it as a
closure --- so one source can \emph{record} a run and another \emph{replace} it,
while the commitments, the lincheck and the openings stay production code.

\paragraph{Make the model executable where feasible.} Random-oracle programming
is usually a sentence in a proof. Here it is an object: a programmable oracle
whose unprogrammed behaviour is byte-identical to the real challenger, against
which the unmodified verifier accepts or does not. This converts a class of
reasoning errors into test failures.

\paragraph{Prefer the witness-free statement.} When the relation has a unique
witness, witness-indistinguishability is vacuous. Restating coverage in
claim-kernel form --- quantified over masks, modulo the public claims --- is both
the only available statement and a strictly stronger one, and it removes the
universal quantifier over witness pairs that no amount of sampling could close.

\paragraph{Write down what breaks, not only what works.} The three failed
simulator constructions, the pinning trap, the padding-digest correction and
the desynchronised nonce are recorded in the design log because each was a
plausible-looking step that measurement rejected. A development that records
only its successes cannot be audited.

\section{What this rests on}
\label{sec:limits}

The composition of the sections above is a rigorous prose argument over
measured and cited components; it is not a machine-checked theorem. Quoting the
result without these conditions would overstate it.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sibling hiding is a random-oracle assumption}, not a derivation.
\item \textbf{Coverage is measured at sampled challenge tuples}, not all of
them. Random probing can only under-estimate a rank, so a full-rank reading is
sound evidence of surjectivity at the tuple measured.
\item \textbf{The opening interior} is carried by the existing low-mask and
blinder lemmas rather than measured at this relation.
\item \textbf{The extractor's ROM-observation half is cited}, not executed;
making it executable means routing leaf hashing through the recording oracle.
\item \textbf{Simulation-extractability is not proved.} Plain zero knowledge
and plain knowledge soundness do not compose in the random-oracle model; the
target is the weak form, with divergence at the statement absorption as the
mechanism.
\item \textbf{No quantitative extraction bound} is stated.
\item Scope: 64-byte messages, the certified configuration ($m=22$, 256
instances), classical ROM. Longer messages need chunk and tree chaining;
QROM and side channels are out of scope.
\item \textbf{No independent cryptographic review} has been performed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Status}

For the certified configuration, and subject to \S\ref{sec:limits}, the
fixed-digest mode is a computational zero-knowledge argument of knowledge in the
classical random-oracle model for knowledge of BLAKE3 preimages of public
digests. Both halves of the property are implemented and tested against the
unmodified verifier, and the tests that would detect their absence are present
and fail when they should.
